Understanding Disabilities and Digital Barriers

Persons with disabilities have different abilities, preferences, and support needs. Digital services should be designed so that everyone can access information and use technology.

1. Visual Disabilities

Some people may have difficulty seeing small text, images, or screen content. Screen readers, magnification, and high-contrast settings can make digital information easier to access.

2. Hearing Disabilities

People who are deaf or hard of hearing may find audio-only content difficult to access. Captions, transcripts, and visual alerts can help.

3. Physical Disabilities

Some people may find it difficult to use a mouse, keyboard, or touchscreen. Keyboard navigation, voice control, and alternative input devices may help.

4. Speech Disabilities

Some people may experience barriers when using services that depend on spoken communication. Text-based communication and alternative communication tools can provide other ways to interact.

5. Cognitive and Learning Disabilities

Complicated instructions, confusing layouts, and long forms may create difficulties. Clear headings, simple language, and consistent navigation can make websites easier to understand.

Common Digital Barriers

  • Websites that are difficult to navigate.
  • Images without useful text descriptions.
  • Videos without captions.
  • Small text and low colour contrast.
  • Online forms with unclear instructions.

Four Principles of Digital Accessibility

Perceivable

Information should be available in ways users can perceive.

Operable

Users should be able to navigate and operate website controls.

Understandable

Information and navigation should be clear and easy to understand.

Robust

Digital content should work with different browsers and assistive technologies.

How Can We Help?

  • Design accessible websites and applications.
  • Spread awareness about assistive technology.
  • Use clear language and readable text.
  • Listen to the needs and feedback of persons with disabilities.
