Digital Accessibility Tools

Assistive technology and accessibility features help persons with disabilities access digital information and use devices according to their individual needs.

1. Screen Readers

Screen readers read screen content aloud or provide information through a Braille display. They can help people with visual impairments use websites, applications, and documents.

Examples: NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver, and TalkBack.

2. Screen Magnification

Magnification tools enlarge text, icons, and other screen content. They may help people who have difficulty seeing small text.

Example: Windows Magnifier and built-in phone magnification.

3. Voice Typing

Voice typing converts spoken words into written text. It can provide another way to enter text without typing every word manually.

4. Captions and Transcripts

Captions display spoken dialogue and relevant sounds as text. Transcripts provide a written version of audio content. These features make audio and video information more accessible.

5. Keyboard Navigation

Keyboard navigation allows users to move through website links and controls without using a mouse. The Tab key often moves between controls, while Enter or Space activates selected controls.

6. Text-to-Speech

Text-to-speech converts written words into spoken audio. It can help people who prefer listening to text or need an alternative way to access written information.

7. Built-in Accessibility Features

  • Screen readers for spoken access to screen content.
  • Magnification for enlarging content.
  • Captions for supported audio and video.
  • Display settings for adjusting text and contrast.
  • Voice typing for entering text by speaking.

How to Find Accessibility Settings

  1. Open Settings on your phone or computer.
  2. Look for the Accessibility section.
  3. Explore the available features.
  4. Read the instructions before enabling a feature.
  5. Choose options that suit your needs.
